---
title: Modes
description: Read-only, comment-only, and editing, and what each lets a reader do.
---

A document opens read-only, for commenting only, or for editing, and the `mode` prop chooses which.
[Props](/docs/props#mode) describes the prop itself.

## Read-only

A read-only document takes no edits and no comments.
It has no toolbar and no context menus.
The text can still be selected and copied, and the comments the document carries can be read.

## Comment-only

The body of a comment-only document cannot be changed, while its text can be selected and copied.
Comments can be added, edited, deleted, replied to, resolved, and reopened.
There is no toolbar, and the right-click menu offers Copy and Add comment only.
Undo and redo take back a comment but not a body edit, from the keyboard as well as from the panel.
A formatting shortcut does nothing.

## Editing

An editing document takes everything, and the built-in toolbar stands above the paper unless `toolbar: false` leaves it out.
The right-click menu adds Cut, Paste, and Delete, and with `locking: true` it also offers Lock and Unlock.
Zoom, page guides, and the comment panel are available in every mode, though the toolbar's zoom picker appears only here.

## Switching modes

The mode can change on an open document, and so can the comment author and whose comments may be edited.
The editor switches in place and keeps the view, its history, and the application's own plugins.
Nothing about the mode is written to the saved file, and a document protection the file already carries is preserved as it is.

## Your comments and other people's

Every comment written here records its author.
Edit and Delete are offered on your own comments, and on comments that carry no recorded identity, such as ones written in Word or written before identities were recorded.
An application can open every comment to everyone with `editableComments: "all"`.
Replying, resolving, and reopening are open to every commenter, and deleting a comment deletes its replies.
[Who may edit a comment](/docs/editor-api/comments-and-notes#who-may-edit-a-comment) states the rule precisely.

## Locked content

A lock is a content control in the document, so it travels with the file.
A lock the document carries is honored in every mode and no prop turns it off, so locked text cannot be edited.
Putting locks on and taking them off is offered only while editing with `locking: true`, from the right-click menu.
Locking works on a stretch of selected text, one control per paragraph the selection runs through, and a caret touching a locked control's edge is enough to unlock it.
[Locked content](/docs/editor-api/modes-and-locks#locked-content) covers the rest.

[Modes and locks](/docs/editor-api/modes-and-locks) in the Editor API covers how a control of your own reads the mode.
