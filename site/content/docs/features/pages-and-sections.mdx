title: Pages and sections
description: Page boundaries, section breaks, and headers and footers.

## Page boundaries

Visual page boundaries are approximate and may differ from Word or from printed output.

Keep with next is reflected in the boundaries the editor draws.
Keep lines together and widow control are kept in the file but are not.

## Sections

Every section a document is written in is read: its paper and margins, the header and footer
stories it selects, and the page number it starts at.

A section break starts a new page unless it is marked continuous, in which case the section after
it carries on down the same page.

The paragraph carrying a section break keeps it.
That paragraph cannot be joined away, and pressing Enter in it keeps the break on the last
paragraph of the section rather than putting it on both halves.

A selection running across the last paragraph of a section cannot be deleted, even when the
section break is not what you meant to remove.
Move the selection clear of that paragraph's boundary to edit the text around it.

## Headers and footers

Each section's plain-text first, even, and default headers and footers are previewed on the page,
including decimal `PAGE` and `NUMPAGES` fields.
A header or footer is shown the way the document body is, though editing one is not offered yet.

## Limitations

Every page is drawn at the width of the first section's paper.
A page is as tall as the paper of the section its own text sits in, so a landscape section is
paginated on landscape pages but still drawn at the first section's width.

An even-page or odd-page section start is kept and goes back out untouched, but it is drawn as an
ordinary new page rather than skipping to a page of that side.

A page number begins at the start its own section declares, but the count does not restart at a
section boundary: a later section's number carries on from the page's place in the whole document.

Which header and footer a page shows - first, even, or default - is chosen from the page's place in
its own section, so every section's own first page is drawn as a first page.
