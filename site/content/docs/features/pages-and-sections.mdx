title: Pages and sections
description: Page boundaries, section breaks, and headers and footers.

## Page boundaries

Page boundaries are approximate and may differ from Word or from print.
A page ends above the footnotes its text refers to.
Keep with next is reflected in them; keep lines together and widow control are kept in the file
but not drawn.
Keep with next is ignored when the kept blocks cannot fit on one page, and never joins a table to
the block after it.

## Sections

Every section is read with its paper and margins, its headers and footers, and its starting page
number.
A section break starts a new page unless it is marked continuous.
An edit that removes a paragraph carrying a section break is refused without a message; move the
selection clear of it to edit the text around it.
Section breaks have no visible marker.

## Headers and footers

Each section's plain-text first, even, and default headers and footers are previewed on the page,
including decimal `PAGE` and `NUMPAGES` fields.
Editing a header or footer is not offered yet.

## Limitations

Every page is drawn at the width of the first section's paper; a landscape section is paginated on
its own height but drawn at that width.

Footnotes that do not fit at the foot of their page move whole to the foot of the next page, and a
footnote taller than a page stays on its page and scrolls within the room that page has.
Endnotes that do not fit after the last paragraph run on down pages of their own, which carry the
last section's header and footer.

A custom separator a document defines is drawn as the default line.

A list inside a footnote or an endnote is drawn without its markers and hanging indent, and a
tab inside one at the default width.

An even-page or odd-page section start is kept in the file but drawn as an ordinary new page.

Page numbering after a restart in a later section is wrong, and even and odd header selection is
counted from the section rather than from the document, so the two can disagree in any section
after the first.

Every section's own first page is drawn as a first page for the choice of header and footer.
